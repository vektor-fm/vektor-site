Claude Code · Sellbird

It reads their site
before it writes a word.

Sellbird (formerly Harvey) is an open-source sales agent that runs in Claude Code. You tell it what you sell and who your dream customer is. It finds local businesses and reads every one of their websites with no model call, then Claude scores who fits, writes each one an email about a real detail from their site, and every email waits for your yes. Below: the repo, the setup, what it really costs and the cold-email rules.

What it does, step by step.

The pipeline as the README describes it. You can run it end to end, or stop at any step.

1Find companies. Free on OpenStreetMap by default, or on a paid source you pick; every paid search shows its cost before it runs.
2Read their websites. Three plain HTTP requests per business, checked for the buying signals you confirmed (no online booking, ads running, a role they're hiring for). No model calls, no cost.
3Score and write. Claude scores each prospect against your ideal customer and writes a short email about something it found on their site.
4Wait for your yes. Every outgoing email waits in the Outbox for your approval until you turn on autopilot yourself. A pre-send check with no model rejects banned phrases, unfilled merge tags, overlong emails and extra links.
5Handle replies. It reads every reply, drafts an answer and honours opt-outs at once. It wakes every 15 minutes on your own machine, inside quiet hours, a daily limit and the recipient's office hours.
6Watch it. sellbird dashboard opens a private dashboard at 127.0.0.1:5555.

What it really costs.

"No model API bill" is true. "Free" is not the whole story. From the README's cost table and docs/deliverability.md, checked 2026-11-18.

1The model: your plan. It thinks through the Claude Code CLI on your Claude Pro or Max plan (or Codex on a ChatGPT plan). No API key, no per-token billing; it throttles itself to leave room for your own work. Your plan's usage limits still apply.
2Finding and reading: $0 on the default source. The cheapest paid source is about $0.37 per 1,000 businesses, and optional.
3A mailbox: about $7 a month for Google Workspace. Microsoft 365 and SMTP work too.
4A separate sending domain and 2 to 4 weeks of warm-up before real volume, with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up. Cold email from your main domain, or at volume on day one, lands in spam.

Fig. 04 ·

You are the sender.

Sellbird automates the outreach; the law applies to you. From its own legal and deliverability page.

1EU and UK (GDPR / PECR). Business cold email needs a defensible legitimate interest: the pitch must matter to that person's role, you must know where the data came from, and objecting must be effortless.
2Some places want consent first. Germany wants prior consent even for business email; a UK sole trader needs consent where a limited company doesn't; Canada's rules depend on whether the address was published.
3Keep the defaults. Approval mode, the daily limit and office hours are on out of the box. Ramp slowly: 10 to 20 a day per inbox at the start.

Fig. 05 ·

Know what you're adding.

Read this before you rely on it.

1We haven't run it. Every step on this page and in the film comes from the repo's README and docs. The Portland bakery email in the film is the source creator's illustration, not a real send.
2It doesn't book meetings. It drafts your answers to replies; you book the call. Calendar auto-booking is on the repo's roadmap, not built.
3It runs while your machine does. It is a loop on your own computer, not a hosted service, and it sleeps in quiet hours.
4The licence is AGPL-3.0. Free to run yourself; obligations apply if you modify it and offer it as a network service. Harvey releases up to 0.3.x were MIT.
