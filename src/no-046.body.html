AI models · What to actually do

Four things to do
with Fable 5.1.

Anthropic shipped Fable 5.1 on 1 September 2026. The benchmark table is not the reason to open it. The reason is that a model you last used six months ago is now good enough to be pointed at your own work and told to be honest about it. Below: the four moves, the prompt for each one written out, and the one number that changed the arithmetic.

Fig. 01 ·

Point it at something you already built.

Not a toy. A thing that exists and that you care about: an app, a site, a contract, a deck.

Ask it to be three readers, not one

“What is wrong with this” gets you small, safe notes. Instead: read this as a hostile reviewer, then as a new hire on day one, then as the person maintaining it in two years — and label which one you are being.

The three answers will contradict each other. The contradictions are the findings.

Then: rank everything by what it costs me to leave it alone.

A crash nobody explained in four to five years

In Anthropic's launch material the investment firm Millennium describe a rare crash — about one in a million runs — that no engineer on the team, and no model they tried, had explained in four to five years. Fable 5.1 found the cause.

Same instruction as above, aimed at a bug instead of a document. Source: the Fable and Mythos 5.1 announcement, anthropic.com.

The line that does the work

End the prompt with “Do not be encouraging. I am not asking whether it is good.” Without it you get a compliment and three suggestions.

Ten minutes. It tells you whether the other three are worth your afternoon.

Fig. 02 ·

Build an agent. It can be small.

Most people still have not built one, which is fair — until recently they were flaky. That stopped being true this year.

Read → do → check → read

That loop is the whole definition. An agent that cannot check its own work is a script with extra steps.

Write the loop out explicitly in the prompt: what it reads, what it does, how it verifies, and what it does when the check fails.

The failure branch is the part people leave out, and it is the part that decides whether you can leave it running.

Pick a job you do on a schedule and resent

Say it in one sentence, then state when it runs, what it can reach, and the observable state that means it is finished. Ask it to interview you before it writes anything.

Vague finish condition is the number one reason a first agent never gets switched on.

Start it read-only

Give it the ability to look and to propose. Hold back the ability to change anything until you have watched a few runs.

You are buying the right to be wrong cheaply.

Fig. 03 ·

Describe the hardest problem you have not solved.

The one you have been avoiding. In detail, the whole thing. This is the move people skip because it feels like it will not work.

Give it the failures, not just the problem

What you already tried, and why each attempt failed, is what turns this from a horoscope into an analysis. Leave it out and you get the three obvious suggestions you already rejected.

Include what you are afraid the real answer is. It changes the reply.

Make it play the problem back first

What do you think the actual problem is, as opposed to the one I described? Say it plainly even if it is unflattering. Then, and only then, three moves you could make this week — with the cost of each one being wrong.

“Do not motivate me” belongs in this prompt too.

It does not have to be a coding problem

Pricing. A hire you keep postponing. A partnership that is not working. Length is a feature here — this is the one prompt where a page of context beats a tidy paragraph.

Of the four, this is the one nobody does.

Fig. 04 ·

Experiment with effort levels.

The pricing moved, so the arithmetic moved with it.

Cache reads fell 75%

To $0.25 per million tokens — which Anthropic puts at roughly a quarter off a typical workload and close to half off an agentic one.

Figures from the Fable and Mythos 5.1 announcement, anthropic.com, 1 September 2026.

Sort your jobs into four buckets

High volume and low judgement · drafting a human will edit · anything where being wrong is expensive · long agentic runs. Ask which tier and effort setting each one actually needs, and what specifically breaks one step cheaper.

The answer to that second half is the only part worth reading.

Caching is what people leave on the table

In a long agentic run the same context is read over and over. That is exactly the read the price cut applies to — so the saving is largest precisely where the bill was worst.
