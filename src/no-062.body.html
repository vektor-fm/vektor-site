Maths for life · the odds table

Throw the dice
ten times.

One throw of a dice gives a 16.7% chance of a six. Ten throws give 83.8%. On paper, enough throws make anything likely. In real life you get a few throws, the dice is your idea and the tray is your market. Below: the odds table for a few throws at 1 in 6, 1 in 20 and 1 in 100, what it quietly assumes, and the four things to rework first, before you swap the dice.

Fig. 02 ·

What the table assumes.

Four conditions sit under every number above. Real life breaks each one.

1The same dice every throw. The table only holds if every throw has the same odds. Swap the idea after a miss and you trade a dice you know has a six for one you don't: it might be worse, or have no six at all. Changing the idea after one miss has a name: shiny object syndrome.
2The dice has a six on it. A wrong idea is a dice with no six: 0% at any number of throws. More throws do not fix it.
3The tray holds the dice. A market too small, or a problem nobody has, is a tray the dice slides off. The throw never counts.
4Each throw is independent. Real attempts are not: each one teaches you something. Keep the dice, change the throw, and later throws can land more often than the first, so the table is a floor. Change everything at once and you learn nothing about which change mattered.

Fig. 03 ·

Rework the throw first.

Before you swap the dice (the idea) or the tray (the market), rework the four parts of the throw the film names, in this order.

1 · The product

Does it do the one job it promises, for the person it is for? Rework where people stall, not where you would like to add something.

2 · The offer

What exactly they get, who it is for, and why now, in one sentence. The same product can miss on one offer and land on another.

3 · The price

The number and the model: one-off, subscription, free tier. Change one of them per throw.

4 · The channel

Where the people who have the problem find you. Give one channel enough throws to read a result before you switch.

One rule across all four: change one thing per throw, so you can tell which one moved the result.
