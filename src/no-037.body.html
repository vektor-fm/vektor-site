7. Perplexity

The internet is full of noise. Perplexity answers with sources attached, not just a guess — you get the citation, not just the claim. That's the whole case for it over a plain chatbot: when you need to check something, not just be told something, it shows its working.

6. Fathom

Barely anyone uses it, and it just sits in your meetings taking the notes for you. No prompting, no setup mid-call — it's running in the background while you're actually talking, which is the entire pitch: the tool you forget is there is the one that saves the most time.

5. Make

The easier version of the same idea as our #1 pick. If you're just starting out with automation and n8n's node graph looks like a lot on day one, Make is the on-ramp — same "connect your apps, skip the manual work" logic, gentler learning curve.

4. NotebookLM

Upload your PDFs, your docs, your YouTube videos — it turns your own material into summaries, presentations, even a podcast. Not a general chatbot: it works from your source material specifically, which is the opposite failure mode to ChatGPT's confident guessing. A naming note ↓

3. Gamma

Clean, professional presentations in minutes, no design skill required. The pitch is narrow on purpose: one job — turn an idea or an outline into a deck that doesn't look like a first draft — done well, instead of a chatbot doing it as an afterthought.

2. Gemini

It's getting good enough that what looked impossible a year ago is normal now. Second place isn't a hedge — it's the closest thing on this list to a genuine ChatGPT alternative, and the gap that used to justify picking one over the other has been closing fast.

1. n8n

Not a chatbot — an automation platform. If you want to make real money with AI this year, automation is the skill: n8n is the tool that turns "I asked an AI" into "I built something that runs itself." That's the whole argument for putting it above every model on this list, including the one everyone defaults to.

Why ChatGPT isn't here

Not a claim that it's bad — a claim that it's not the first tool that saves you hours if you don't already have one. Every tool above does one specific job well: sourced answers, meeting notes nobody typed, automation, working from your own files, decks, and the model that's closing the gap fastest. ChatGPT is the generalist most people already have open; this list is the seven that do something it doesn't do as well, or doesn't do at all.
