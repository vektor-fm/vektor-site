The SIX page · the comparison

Six AI agents compared.

Dots, Muse, Grok Bot, OpenClaw, Hermes and MausBot: where they run, what they do and what the research establishes about cost. MausBot is the manager layer for coding agents you already use.

Compare the agents ↓The manager pattern ↓

The comparison ·

Which job do you need done?

Product descriptions and prices below come from the research recorded on 2026-10-30. Missing prices stay marked. “Your machine” describes where the app or agent runs; the research does not establish that its models run locally.

OpenAI Dots

Runs: Cloud, with its own computer and browser.

For: Ongoing responsibilities inside ChatGPT. It works between conversations and can use supported plugins enabled for your account.

Cost: Available on the Pro 100, Pro 200 and Pro 500 plans. The official page gives no dollar price. see the product page.

Link: Dots documentation

The recorded availability is Pro 100/200/500 outside the EEA, UK and Switzerland; Business Premium and Enterprise were rolling out worldwide.

Meta Muse

Runs: Cloud, on its own dedicated computer.

For: Everyday tasks such as booking travel, buying things, filling out forms and turning longer-term goals into action plans.

Cost: Meta describes it as “free for most of what people need”. Exact limits and any paid pricing are unverified; see the product page.

Link: Meta’s Muse introduction

Grok Bot

Runs: Cloud, where the bots share a computer.

For: A team of always-on agents with specialist roles for sales, marketing, office operations and bug fixes, with a chief of staff above them.

Cost: No price is listed. It is in beta for SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus and SuperGrok Heavy subscribers, and for Cursor Pro, Pro+, Ultra and Teams subscribers. see the product page.

Link: Grok Bot introduction

OpenClaw

Runs: Your machine, running in the background after a terminal setup.

For: An open-source assistant you can message through chat apps such as Telegram or WhatsApp.

Cost: Open source under MIT. The project says it has no paid tier, hosted service or token. It does not state what the model you connect will cost. see the product page.

Link: OpenClaw on GitHub

Hermes Agent

Runs: Your machine, through its desktop app or command-line agent.

For: Creates skills from experience and improves them during use. Profiles appear as bots with different roles, models and skills, and can deliberate in group chats.

Cost: Open source under MIT. The repository states no price or hosting cost. see the product page.

Link: Hermes Agent on GitHub

MausBot

Runs: Your machine. Its desktop app runs the coding agents already installed there.

For: A manager layer for Claude Code, Codex and Grok, bringing them into a shared window as bots under a lead.

Cost: The desktop and companion apps are free and open source under Apache-2.0. The site lists cloud plans at $15 / $49 / $99 a month. The README says hosted/mobile connectivity is still being built. Cloud availability, plan inclusions and underlying agent/model costs are unverified; see the product page.

Link: milind-soni/OpenMausBot on GitHub

mausbot.com

Logos belong to their respective owners and are shown to identify the products.

The pattern ·

A manager for your agents.

An orchestrator is a manager layer. It runs the coding agents you already use as bots under a lead, so they work as a team in a shared window. In the pattern shown in the film, every command a bot wants to run comes back to you as an allow or deny card on your desktop.

MausBot is the open-source example: Claude Code, Codex and Grok appear as bots beneath a Chief of Staff. Its project is milind-soni/OpenMausBot, licensed under Apache-2.0. Its site is mausbot.com.

That is a description of the documented workflow, not a tested guarantee about command approvals.
