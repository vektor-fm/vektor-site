# Copilot or autopilot? (the NO.091 prompt)

The keyword payload for NO.091 "Sell the job, not the tool". Paste the block below into Claude (claude.ai, the Claude
app or Claude Code), replace the idea line, send. It reads your AI product idea through Julien Bek's Sequoia essay
"Services: The New Software" (published 2026-03-05) and tells you which half you are building.

Test run: `test-run.md` beside this file (https://vektor-fm.github.io/vektor-site/files/no-091/test-run.md) (input: an AI tool that drafts replies to Shopify support tickets; run
2026-10-30 with `claude -p --model sonnet --strict-mcp-config`, no tools).

---

```
Read my AI product idea through one lens: Julien Bek's Sequoia essay "Services: The New Software" (March 2026).
His argument, in short: a copilot sells the tool and an autopilot sells the work; companies spend far more on
services (people doing the job) than on the software around the job; and the jobs that fall to autopilots first are
the ones that are mostly intelligence work (routine, well defined, often outsourced today) rather than judgement work
(deciding what to do and owning the risk, where the customer keeps a human accountable).

My AI product idea:
<IDEA>

Answer in four short parts, plain prose, one paragraph each, no bullet lists, under 250 words in total.

The job. Name the human service this product sits next to, who does that job today (a role, an outsourced firm or
an agency), and roughly what a company pays for that work compared with the software tools around it. Give ranges,
say they are rough estimates, and never state a precise statistic or cite a source you cannot name.

The line. Place the job on Bek's intelligence-to-judgement line: mostly intelligence, mostly judgement, or split.
Say which parts of the job are which.

The verdict. Say in one sentence whether my idea, as stated, is a copilot (sells the tool, a person still does the
job) or an autopilot (sells the finished job), then describe the autopilot version of the same idea in one or two
sentences.

The offer. Write one outcome-priced offer line a customer would read, shaped like "we [finished job] every [period
or unit] for [a price shape]". No promised revenue or return for me, and no dollar figure you cannot defend as a
rough range.
```
